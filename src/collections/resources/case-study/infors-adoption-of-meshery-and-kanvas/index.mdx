## The problem they set out to solve

Infor's business is industry-specific software delivered as cloud suites — which means its platform teams carry an unusual burden. They support product engineering organizations building for dozens of micro-verticals, each with its own compliance expectations, data residency requirements, and release cadence. Infrastructure that serves one industry well can be wrong for another, and tribal knowledge about "how our cloud actually fits together" calcifies in the heads of a few senior engineers.

Three needs drove the search for a shared plane:

1. **A shared design surface** — platform teams across product lines needed one place to see, discuss, and agree on infrastructure — visually, not as walls of YAML in chat threads.
2. **Multi-tenancy by default** — isolation between product lines and customer environments is a business requirement, not a nice-to-have.
3. **An open foundation** — Infor differentiates on industry software, not on internal plumbing. Whatever sits underneath the platform has to be inspectable, extensible, and free of vendor cul-de-sacs.

## Where Meshery sits in the stack

Meshery is Infor's **design and collaboration plane**: the surface where platform engineers model cloud-native infrastructure as visual designs, review changes together, and keep a shared system of record for how the platform fits together. Like the TCS Labs engagement, the deployment itself is hosted by Layer5 as a multi-tenant environment, so Infor's platform team spends its time building on Meshery — not operating the control plane underneath it.

The engagement mirrors the TCS Labs model: Layer5 operates the hosted Meshery deployment while Infor's engineers extend it, contribute upstream, and fold it into their platform workflows.

## Why Meshery won

For a company whose product is industry-specific detail, generic tooling was never going to be enough. Several things drove the choice:

- **Collaboration as a first-class concern** — infrastructure design happens the way product work happens: together, on a shared surface, with review built in.
- **A visual abstraction over cloud-native complexity** — Kanvas lets engineers see components, relationships, and configuration as one connected diagram instead of disconnected text files.
- **Model-driven breadth** — Meshery's models incorporate other abstraction layers, so adopting it never meant abandoning existing investments.
- **No lock-in** — designs export in multiple formats, which made the choice defensible at every level of review.
- **Open core and CNCF-native** — contributable, in Go and TypeScript, with a community Infor's engineers could join rather than just consume.

## The champion: from Infor engineer to Meshery maintainer

Every enterprise adoption has a human origin story. At Infor, it is Pontus Ringblom — a platform engineer in Infor's cloud organization who didn't just adopt Meshery, but joined the project building it. Pontus is a Meshery Extensions maintainer for **shape-builder**, the Meshery extension for creating custom polygon shapes for Kanvas components: the tool that lets anyone define the visual language their infrastructure speaks. When Infor needed something that didn't exist, its champion built it upstream — and now maintains it for the whole community.

## The Layer5 engagement: hosted multi-tenant Meshery

Infor's Meshery deployment is hosted by Layer5 — the same service model as the TCS Labs engagement. Tenant boundaries follow the organization's structure, isolation is the default, and because Layer5 operates the control plane, Infor's platform team focuses on extending Meshery and embedding it in their workflows rather than running it.

## Open by default: contributing upstream

Infor's edge in the engagement isn't secrecy — it's participation. Through Pontus's maintainership of shape-builder, Infor contributes to the Meshery extension ecosystem in the open: the polygon-shape tooling its engineers rely on is the same tooling the whole community uses. That is the open-core bargain working as intended — the enterprise gets influence and velocity, the project gets battle-tested contributions from production use.
